## The setup before

Deccan Polymers manufactures specialty engineering polymers for the automotive, agricultural, and electrical industries. Scale: mid-sized, ~60 shop-floor staff, ~₹42 Cr annual turnover, 14 finished-good SKUs, extensive job-work partnerships for secondary operations.

What was working: the accounting layer. TallyPrime Gold on-prem, clean bookkeeping, GST compliance in order.

What was not working: production costing. The CEO could tell you monthly P&L to the rupee, but *per-SKU* profitability was a quarterly-at-best exercise, done in Excel from Tally extracts, with assumptions that drifted over time. Pricing decisions were being made on hunch.

Two specific issues compounded:

1. **BOM model was incomplete.** Multi-stage sub-assemblies were collapsed into single BOMs, hiding yield losses at intermediate stages.
2. **Job-work was off-book.** Material-out, material-in, and value-addition were tracked in spreadsheets because the Tally voucher flow felt too rigid.

## What we deployed

**Phase 1 (weeks 1–2): BOM audit + rebuild**
We walked every finished good from raw material through to despatch. 38 sub-assembly BOMs were documented; 14 multi-level BOMs for finished goods were rebuilt. Cost roll-up was configured to honour the new structure.

**Phase 2 (weeks 3–4): Job-work vouchers**
The Manufacturing vertical pack's job-work vouchers were configured — material-out, material-in, value-addition, job-worker settlement — all on the same reconciliation screen. Historical spreadsheets were validated against Tally and archived.

**Phase 3 (weeks 5–6): Production costing TDL**
Custom TDL built for per-batch production cost sheets. Each production-order closure now produces: material cost (at standard + actual), labour cost (allocation rule), overhead (activity-based), variance against standard. Output ships to the CEO's email as a PDF.

**Phase 4 (week 7): Training + go-live**
Shop-floor supervisors, production planners, and the costing team all received role-specific training. The CEO got a 90-minute walk-through of the monthly cost sheet format.

## The outcome

After three closed production cycles:

- **Per-batch cost accuracy**: tightened from ±22% (Excel-era) to ±3% (Tally-native)
- **Pricing reviews**: the CEO now runs a monthly pricing review using real per-SKU margin data. Six SKUs were repriced in the first quarter.
- **Yield visibility**: sub-assembly yield losses, previously invisible, are now reported weekly. Three yield-improvement projects started.
- **Audit cycle**: statutory auditor's working-paper prep time halved — they now self-serve from the job-work reconciliation screen.
